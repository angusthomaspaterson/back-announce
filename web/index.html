Back Announce gathers the latest episodes and articles from radio shows, mixes, podcasts and music writers into one timeline, plus what's live right now on the radio. Every show in it was chosen by a person. Nothing is recommended by an algorithm.

Make it yours

  • You start with Angus's feed. Tap Shows at the end of the row of chips to see every show in the catalogue, each with a switch. Switch off what you don't want, and switch on anything he has paused.
  • On that screen, the search box finds a show by name. New lists shows added in the last three weeks.
  • Pause show on a card does the same as its switch: the show leaves your feed until you switch it back on. To bring one back, tap paused at the end of the filter row: the latest you paused is at the top.
  • The coloured chips (NTS, BBC, Bass and so on) narrow the feed: tap triple j to see only triple j, tap another to add it, and tap All to see everything again. Nothing is paused. ▶ Plays here shows only episodes you can play inside the app.
  • Know a show that belongs here? Suggest a show, on the Shows screen, sends Angus the link.

The feed

  • Newest first, under day headings in your own time. New marks anything since you last pressed Mark all seen.
  • Tap a card's text to read all of it. Where a station publishes its tracklist, it's there.
  • Tap a show's name to see what it's about and its latest episodes. Back returns you to the same spot.
  • Search (the magnifying glass, top right) looks through every recent episode's title, description and tracklist, and every show. Try an artist, a DJ or a label.
  • Open goes to the episode or article; the button says where, such as Open in NTS. On Android it opens the NTS, BBC Sounds, ABC listen, SoundCloud, Mixcloud or Spotify app if you have it.
  • Save (the heart) keeps a copy in your Saved list, so it stays after the episode drops out of the feed. Share sends the episode to someone.
  • Angus's picks are episodes he has singled out, each with a note on why.

Listening here

  • ▶ Play plays the episode in the player at the bottom, where the audio allows it: podcasts, triple j and Double J, Rinse FM and SoundCloud shows among them. Your phone's lock screen shows it too.
  • Stop part way and the button says Resume with the time; it picks up where you left off. A thin bar on the card shows how far you got, and Played ✓ marks one you finished.
  • + Up next lines an episode up to play after the current one. The player shows what's next.
  • In the player, −15s and +30s skip, and the speed button cycles through 1×, 1.25×, 1.5×, 2× and 0.75×.

Take it with you

  • Take it to another device, on the Shows screen and in Settings, makes a link and a QR code that carry your shows and Saved cards. Open it on your other phone or computer.
  • Share my feed makes a link to the shows you follow, for a friend to try.
  • Install it: Settings has an Install button where your browser allows it. Otherwise, in Chrome or Edge use Install in the browser's menu; on an iPhone use Safari's Share button, then Add to Home Screen. It then opens like any other app.

On Air

  • On now with time left, Next 24 hours, and This week by day, all in your own time.
  • Listen live opens the station's own player. Play here plays it in the app where the station allows it.

Your privacy

There is no account. Your shows, Saved cards, listening places and seen marker are kept in this browser, on this device, and are never uploaded. Clearing the browser's data removes them, so use Take it to another device if you want a copy.

Visits are counted anonymously with GoatCounter, so Angus can see roughly how many people use the app and which screens they open. It sets no cookies, keeps no IP addresses and can't tell who you are. It never records which shows or episodes you follow, save or play.

About

Back Announce was made by Angus Paterson, a former music journalist in Perth, to keep up with the radio shows, mixes and writers he follows without an algorithm choosing for him. It reads each show's own public feed or page once a day and links back to it. Every episode plays from, or opens at, the show's own home, and the credit stays with the people who made it.

Run a show listed here and would rather it wasn't? Or spotted something wrong? Message Angus on LinkedIn and he'll sort it.

Set up each device once

  1. Install it. On the Pixel, open the site in Chrome, tap the three dots, then Add to Home screen and Install. On the laptop, use the install icon at the right of Chrome's address bar.
  2. Turn on saving. Tap the Settings button (the two sliders, top right), paste a GitHub token and press Save token. Settings has the steps for making one under "How to make a token". Make a separate token for each device.
  3. Without a token the app works as it does for a visitor: Save, Pause and Mark all seen are kept on that device only and don't reach your other devices.

Feed

  • Newest first, under day headings in Perth time. New marks anything since you last pressed Mark all seen; the number on the Feed tab counts them.
  • Tap a card's text to read all of it. On a First Floor recommendations card this also lists each record with its link.
  • Tap the round initials or the source's name to see everything stored from that source. Back returns you to the same spot.
  • Group chips (Bass, NTS, Writing and so on) narrow the feed: with everything showing, tapping one shows only that group; tap others to add them, and All (or the last one left) shows everything again. This is remembered on this device only and pauses nothing.
  • Open goes to the episode or article. Play plays it in the player at the bottom; the lock screen shows it, with play, pause, back 15 seconds and forward 30. The player also has a speed button, and + Up next on a card queues an episode.
  • Each device remembers where you stopped an episode: the button then says Resume with the time, and a finished one shows Played ✓. This is kept on the device, not in GitHub.
  • Search (the magnifying glass) looks through every recent episode's title, description and tracklist, and every source.
  • Save (the heart) keeps a full copy in Saved, so it stays after the episode drops out of the feed.
  • Pause source hides that source on every device. It is still gathered, so resuming it brings its recent episodes straight back. The paused chip at the end of the filter row opens the Paused list, most recently paused first, where its switch turns it back on.
  • Every Save, Pause and Mark all seen shows a message at the bottom with Undo.
  • Refresh (the circular arrow, top right) fetches the latest feed and any changes from your other device. It also happens by itself when you come back to the app after a few minutes.

Saved

  • Tap the Saved chip at the start of the row. Newest saves first, under the day you saved them.
  • Tap the heart to remove one; Undo brings it back.

Sources

  • Tap the Sources chip at the end of the row. Each source has a switch, and the list starts with the newest episode, so quiet sources sink to the bottom.
  • All, Following, Paused filter the list. To bring one back, tap Paused and switch it on; it stays in view until you change the filter.
  • Sort changes the order: newest episode, recently added, A to Z, or By category, which groups them and adds Pause group and Resume group.
  • Bulk changes, at the top, opens Keep only my original 20, the "added 1 Oct" and "added 11 Oct" buttons and Resume everything.
  • The right-hand column shows the date of the newest item, or failed (the last fetch didn't work, the older items are kept), or link only (the site refused the gatherer, and the card opens the site instead).
  • Tap a name to open that source's page.

On Air

  • On now with time left, Next 24 hours, and This week by day. Everything is in Perth time, and UK, Sydney and Berlin clock changes are handled automatically.
  • Station chips hide or show a station on this device.
  • Listen live opens the station's own player. Play here plays it in the app, and the lock screen shows the show on now, updating when the next one starts.
  • Copy stream copies the address for a radio app. triple j, Double J and FluxFM can't play inside the app; use Listen live for those.

Offline

The app keeps the last feed and your recent pictures, so it opens with no signal. Saves and pauses made offline are sent when the connection is back. Playing audio needs a connection.

When things update

  • The feed is gathered on GitHub once a day, early in the morning. It's set for 3:17 am Perth time, but GitHub often runs it a couple of hours late, so it's usually done by about 6 am. A source you add is gathered within a few minutes.
  • The Substack newsletters (First Floor, No Tags, Futurism Restated, Tone Glow, Erratic Aesthetic, and five you've paused) are gathered by the laptop once a day, at 5:30 am or as soon as it's next on, because Substack turns GitHub away. YouTube does the same, so Underground Live Forever is gathered by the laptop too.
  • The Back Announce page in Claude copies the latest episodes, your Saved list and upcoming releases at about 7 am each day.
  • The app picks up a new gather a few minutes after it finishes.
  • Your saves and pauses reach GitHub a couple of seconds after you tap. The status line says "saving…" until they have.

If something looks wrong

  • The status line says "not saved". Open Settings; it says why. Usually the token has expired, so make a new one and paste it in.
  • The "Updated" time is more than a day old, or a yellow note at the top of the feed says it hasn't refreshed. On GitHub, open the repository's Actions tab and choose Gather the feed. If a yellow banner says it was disabled, press Enable workflow. Then press Run workflow.
  • The Substack sources are behind. The laptop has been off; they catch up next time it's on.
  • A source says "failed" for days. The site may have moved its feed. Ask Claude in the back-announce folder to look into it.
  • You see an old version of the app. Close it fully and open it again.

Adding a source

  • On the Sources screen, tap + Add source and paste a link. Or, in another app such as NTS, SoundCloud, Mixcloud or Apple Podcasts, tap Share and choose Back Announce.
  • Links that work: NTS and Rinse FM shows or episodes, Mixcloud and SoundCloud accounts or tracks, Apple Podcasts shows, any podcast or newsletter feed, Substack newsletters, and magazine websites that publish a feed. Spotify, YouTube and Bandcamp pages don't have feeds.
  • GitHub checks the link, which takes about a minute, then shows the latest episodes. Check the name, pick a group (or make a new one) and tap Add to Back Announce. Its episodes arrive a few minutes later.
  • If you already follow it, the app says so and takes you to its page.
  • To rename, regroup or remove a source, ask Claude in the back-announce folder for now.

Opening links in other apps

  • On the Pixel, links open in their own apps: SoundCloud, Mixcloud, Spotify, NTS, YouTube, KEXP, BBC Sounds, and ABC listen for triple j and Double J. If an app isn't installed, the link opens the website instead.
  • Rinse FM episodes play right here instead: ▶ Play uses the replay Rinse publishes for each show, with resume, Up next and the lock screen. Open still goes to the Rinse website, because the Rinse app doesn't accept links from other apps. For Rinse live, use On Air or open the Rinse app yourself.
  • Podcast episodes and Anjunadeep releases have a Spotify button. It opens Spotify searching for that episode or release, usually with the right one at the top. Mixes, radio shows and writing don't have one, because they are rarely on Spotify.

Visitors and your picks

  • Visitors (anyone without your token) start with your followed shows and can pause, save and mark seen for themselves. Their choices stay in their own browser and never reach you or GitHub. They don't see your Saved list in the app.
  • Pick, on any card, adds it to Angus's picks with your note. Picks are public. Tap Picked ✓ to change the note or remove the pick. The newest 40 are kept.
  • Share my feed, on the Sources screen, makes a link to the shows you follow.
  • To see what a visitor sees, open the site in a private browser window.
  • Visit counts are at backannounce.goatcounter.com: screens opened and actions such as Play or Save, never which show. Devices with your token aren't counted.

Settings

Owner sign-in

A GitHub token makes this one of Angus's own devices: saves, pauses and picks go to the repository and reach his other devices. It is kept on this device only, and only ever sent to GitHub.

How to make a token
  1. On this device, open github.com/settings/personal-access-tokens/new and sign in.
  2. Token name: Back Announce, plus the device, for example Back Announce Pixel.
  3. Expiration: pick the longest option offered, and note the date.
  4. Repository access: choose Only select repositories, then pick back-announce.
  5. Permissions: open Repository permissions, find Contents and set it to Read and write. Leave everything else alone. GitHub adds Metadata (read only) by itself; that is expected.
  6. Press Generate token, copy it, paste it in the box above and press Save token.

Treat it like a password. Make a separate one for each device, so you can cancel one without affecting the other.
